Locksmith or Handyman in Sussex County, DE? Who to Call for Lock Problems

Which lock problems belong to a locksmith, which belong to a handyman, and what to check first.

Locksmith or handyman in Sussex County, DE? Honest answer first. If the trouble is inside the lock, or it has anything to do with a key, call a locksmith. If it's the door, the frame or the paint, that's a handyman's turf. Plenty of folks can stop right there.

Here's what trips people up, though. A lock that "died" is very often a door that dropped a little on its hinges. And a key that won't turn for the handyman is, just as often, a cylinder worn smooth. We get both calls, sometimes in the same week. So before you dial anybody, work out which story is yours. It beats paying for two visits.

Key Takeaways

  • Call a locksmith when the problem is inside the lock or involves a key.
  • Call a handyman when the problem is the door, the frame or the finish.
  • We call that split the Cylinder Line.

Still unsure? Ring a locksmith and just describe it. A few details usually tell us whether the lock or the door is at fault. If it's the door, we'll say so, even though that means the job isn't ours.

What is the Cylinder Line?

We call it the Cylinder Line, and it's the rule of thumb we use on the phone. Think of the cylinder as a line down the middle of the door. The part your key slides into, the pins behind it and any keypad or reader sit on our side. The slab, the hinges, the jamb and the paint sit on theirs. It isn't airtight. Plenty of good handymen can swap a deadbolt without breaking a sweat. It's just a quick way to decide who gets the first call.

Which jobs go to a locksmith?

Keys, cylinders and electronic access. That's our whole day.

  • Lockouts. Getting back into a house, car or business without damage. Our house lockout service page explains how.
  • Rekeying. New pins inside the cylinder, so the new key works and the old ones are dead. We weigh it against replacing in rekey or replace after moving in.
  • Lock repair. Locks that stick, spin or seize. Snapped keys. Worn cylinders. See lock repair.
  • Smart locks. Fitting and programming, covered in smart lock installation starts with your door.
  • Car keys and fobs. Cutting the blade is the easy bit. Programming a transponder takes specialist tools.
  • Master key systems and access control, mostly for rentals and small businesses.

Which jobs go to a handyman?

If the lock is fine and the door isn't, call a handyman.

  • Planing a door that rubs the frame
  • Resetting hinges that sag
  • Fixing a split jamb
  • Cutting a first bore in a door that never had a lock
  • Paint, weatherstripping and sweeps

Wood swells in a humid Delaware summer, and salt air is rough on exterior hardware near the coast. When a deadbolt only sticks in August, we'd blame the door before the lock.

What about jobs that need both?

Some jobs want a carpenter and a locksmith, and the order matters. Fix the door first, because a good lock can't do much in a door that won't close square. Then fit or rekey the lock once the bolt meets the strike. Finally, test it shut, and then test it again. If it only works while you lean on the door, it isn't fixed.

New locks are the classic overlap. A deadbolt is a security device, so grade counts. Locksmith Ledger says that under the ANSI/BHMA system, "Grade 1 being the highest level of performance," and residential locks sit in Grades 2 and 3. Whoever installs yours, ask which grade you're buying. Our lock replacements page shows what we fit.

How do you check who you are hiring?

Whoever you hire, a few habits protect you.

  • Get the plan in writing for anything bigger than a service call. Delaware's Consumer Protection Unit finalized home improvement rules effective November 1, 2023, and the state's summary says "Homeowners may not be asked to sign incomplete contracts." Bigger door and frame work can fall under them.
  • Ask what will happen before it starts. A good tradesperson explains the options first.
  • Check how long they've worked around here, and read recent reviews.
  • Be wary of a drill. Most lockouts open without damage.

We hold to the same bar. You hear the options, you choose, and only then does a tool touch the lock.

Frequently asked questions

Can a handyman change a lock?
Yes, swapping one standard lockset for another is within many handymen's skills. Rekeying, keys, smart locks and anything that fails inside the cylinder are locksmith work.
Is a locksmith the same as a handyman?
No. A locksmith specializes in locks, keys and access systems. A handyman covers a broad range of home repairs. The Cylinder Line is a good way to remember the difference.
Who do I call for a stuck door lock?
Start with a locksmith. If the key turns but the door will not latch, the cause may be the door, and we will tell you if so.
Who should install a deadbolt?
Either can fit one, but a locksmith can also tell you which grade suits the door and set up the keying. Ask for the lock's ANSI/BHMA grade.
How much does a locksmith cost compared with a handyman?
It depends on the job, so we do not quote a figure in an article. Call (302) 271-0155 with a description and we will walk through the options before any work starts.
Can a handyman rekey my locks?
Rekeying needs pinning tools and key codes, so it is locksmith work. See our lock rekey page.

Yes. The van goes to Milford, Millsboro, Seaford, Lewes, Rehoboth Beach and the rest of Kent and Sussex County.
