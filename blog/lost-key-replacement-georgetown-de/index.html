What is the 3-Question Lost Key Check?

The 3-Question Lost Key Check is how we sort out a lost key on the phone. It's our own shorthand, and it takes about a minute.

  1. What does the key open? House, car, shop door, mailbox or padlock. Each has its own fix.
  2. Could someone find it and tell where it fits? A bare key dropped at the beach is low risk. One on a tagged ring, or in a bag with your mail, is not.
  3. Can you get in or drive right now? A spare, a relative with a key, or an unlocked back door changes how fast you need help.

Once you've answered those, the next step is usually clear.

Lost a house key: rekey, replace, or wait?

Start with question two. Picture a key that slid out of your pocket in a parking lot, with nothing on it. Honestly, the chance that somebody finds it, works out which door it opens and tries it is tiny. Plenty of people just check the car and the laundry basket, give it a few days, and let it go.

Change that picture. The ring has a tag with your name, or the key was in a bag with your mail. Now a stranger has both halves of the puzzle, and a rekey is the sensible move. It beats lying awake wondering. A rekey resets the pins inside the lock you already own, so a new key works and the old ones are dead. The hardware stays on the door. If the lock is worn, loose or cracked, replacing it makes more sense, and the locksmith will see which it is the moment they look at it. Our Georgetown page for lost key replacement has the local details.

Renting? Read your lease before you call anyone. Delaware's tenant law, 25 Del. C. § 5509, gives a tenant the right to install a new lock at their own cost, on three conditions:

  • the landlord is notified in writing and given a key
  • the new lock fits the system already in place
  • the installation does not damage the door

Send that notice by email or letter and keep a dated copy. It saves an argument later about who changed what.

Standing outside with no way in? That's a different job. Ask about a house lockout first and rekey once you're inside.

Lost a car key: spare or no spare?

Car keys aren't what they were twenty years ago. A lot of them hold a small chip, and the car's computer has to recognize it before the engine will turn over. So a lost car key is more than a cutting job. The new one is cut to fit the lock, then programmed so the car accepts it.

You have a spare. Good news, this is the easy one. The locksmith copies the spare and programs the copy, and you're back to two working keys. Then ask about wiping the lost key from the car's memory. Most vehicles allow it, and a found key won't start anything after that. Consider car key duplication too, so you never fall to a single key again.

You have no spare. This takes more steps. There's nothing to copy, so the key is built from your vehicle's details. First the locksmith confirms the car is yours. Then the VIN, make, model and year decide which key fits, and it gets cut and programmed. Our guide to losing every car key in Delaware lists the documents to have ready. Can't tell which case you're in? Start with the lost key replacement page.

If you're locked out on top of all this, you've got company. Consumer Reports, citing AAA, reported in 2015 that AAA gets calls from more than 4,000,000 locked-out motorists every year. The same piece says to keep a spare in your wallet or with someone you trust. Fair advice.

What if your papers were on the same ring?

It happens more than you'd think: the keys vanish along with the glovebox registration, or a wallet, or the folder with the title. A locksmith has to check ownership before making a car key, so those papers matter.

If it's the title that's lost, the Delaware DMV has a duplicate title page. Every owner signs form MV213. If it's the registration card, call DMV customer service at (302) 744-2500. Start now. That paperwork may take longer than the key does.

Papers gone but photo ID still in your wallet? Say so when you call. Other proof of ownership might be accepted, and you'd rather hear that before anyone drives out.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do first when I lose a house key in Georgetown, DE?
Check the car seats, the dryer and the pocket of yesterday's jacket. Still nothing? Think about what else was on the ring. An address tag or a bag of mail means a rekey is worth a call that same day. A plain key lost at the grocery store can wait a few days.
Is rekeying better than changing the lock after a lost key?
It depends on the lock. A deadbolt that turns smoothly is a good rekey candidate. One that sticks, rattles in the door or has a cracked body wants replacing, since new pins can't cure a worn-out lock.
Can a locksmith replace a lost car key at my home?
For most cars, yes: the key gets cut and programmed in your driveway. A handful of makes only allow it at a dealer. We tell you that on the first call, before anybody drives out.
Will my lost car key still start the car after a new one is made?
On most vehicles it won't, because programming the new key clears the old one from the car's memory. Confirm it for your make, model and year before work starts, since a few cars behave differently.
Can a renter change the locks in Delaware?
Yes, but tell the landlord first. Delaware's 25 Del. C. § 5509 lets you put in a new lock at your own cost once you've given written notice and a key. The lock has to fit what's already there, and the door can't be damaged. Check the lease too.
What do I need to prove a car is mine?
Photo ID, plus a registration, a title or an insurance card with the car on it. Locksmiths and dealers don't all want the same papers. Ask when you book.
