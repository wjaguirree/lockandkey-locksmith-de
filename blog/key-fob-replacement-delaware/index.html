What is key fob replacement and programming?

Key fob programming is the process of pairing a remote or smart key with a specific vehicle, so the car's computer recognizes it and responds to the lock, unlock, and start commands. Key fob replacement means supplying a new fob and doing that pairing, usually along with cutting a mechanical key blade if the fob has one.

Many modern cars use a transponder chip in the key or fob. The chip sends a code, and the car's immobilizer checks it before the engine will start. That's why a fob that looks identical to yours won't work until it's been programmed to your car.

Dead battery or dead fob? The 3-Check Fob Test

Most fob failures start small. Buttons need two or three presses, the range shrinks, or the car shows a "key not detected" message. A weak battery is a common cause, and it's the quickest thing to rule out first. Many fobs run on a coin cell such as a CR2032, but the number printed on your old battery is the one to match.

We call the quick method below the 3-Check Fob Test. It takes about five minutes and needs no tools beyond a small flat screwdriver.

Check What to do What it tells you
1. Battery Open the fob and read the number on the battery. Replace it with the same type, positive side facing the way the old one did. A weak or wrong-size battery is a frequent reason a fob goes quiet.
2. Shell and contacts Look for cracks, a loose battery holder, green or white residue, or worn button pads. Moisture, a drop, or worn pads can break a fob even with a good battery.
3. Pairing With a fresh battery in place, try the lock button next to the car, then try starting the car. If the battery and shell are fine and it still fails, the fob has likely lost its pairing or the chip is damaged.

If the fob passes checks one and two but fails check three, you've found a programming problem, and that's where a locksmith comes in.

One more thing to rule out is the car itself. The chip in a transponder key has no battery of its own. The car's ignition system powers it, so a completely dead car battery can stop the immobilizer from recognizing a perfectly good key.

Do you need reprogramming after a battery swap?

Sometimes. AAA says replacing a fob battery can mean the fob has to be reprogrammed to the car (AAA Club Alliance, Fixing Key Fob Problems). Often, though, a battery change is all it takes, and the fob keeps its pairing. It just isn't universal.

How we start a fob call: When a fob "stopped working after a battery change," our process is to check the battery and contacts first, then read the car's key data through the OBD-II port to see whether the car still recognizes the fob. Working in that order saves guessing.

Your owner's manual will say whether your model needs a re-sync. If it doesn't cover it, a locksmith can tell you from the make, model, and year before anyone drives out.

What should you do if you lose your only fob?

Lost your only fob? It's stressful, but it's a routine call. The steps below keep you from making it worse.

  1. Check the obvious places first: coat pockets, the car seats, and the spot near the door where you drop keys. Don't leave the car unattended if it may still be unlocked with the fob inside it.
  2. Note the make, model, and year. You'll also need your vehicle's VIN, which is on the dashboard near the windshield and on your registration.
  3. Have proof of ownership ready. A registration, title, or insurance card tied to your name is the usual proof. A reputable locksmith will ask for it.
  4. Call a mobile locksmith rather than arranging a tow. If the car is still drivable, a locksmith can come to you. If the car is locked, tell them that too, because a car unlock service may come first.
  5. Ask how the locksmith handles the old fob so it can't be used with your car. Practices differ by vehicle, and you should get a clear answer.
  6. Get a spare made while the locksmith is there. Duplicating a fob when you already have a working one is far simpler than starting from nothing.

Working at a beach rental or a house you manage? Expect the locksmith to ask for the owner's or property manager's permission before programming a fob there.

Does Delaware's HB 351 affect key fob programming?

Delaware's HB 351 passed the Senate 21 to 0 on June 25, 2026, was signed on July 23, 2026, and took effect that same day (Delaware General Assembly, House Bill 351).

It makes it a crime to knowingly manufacture, sell, transfer, or possess a "vehicle security circumvention device," meaning tools that intercept key fob signals or are used to program or emulate a key. It also requires lost or stolen devices to be reported to police within 48 hours.

The law targets theft. Its exemptions cover people who use these tools for "a lawful and reasonable purpose," and the bill text lists "a locksmith company" among them, alongside law enforcement, licensed dealers, vehicle manufacturers, and mechanic shops (see section 6711(c) in the bill text).

For drivers, the practical point is simple: ask who is programming your key and what they're using. A working locksmith should be able to tell you plainly how they do the job. This is a plain-language summary, not legal advice; the bill text is linked above.

How can you protect your fob from relay theft?

After testing 850 vehicles over ten years, ADAC (Allgemeiner Deutscher Automobil-Club), Germany's largest auto club, reports that about 70% are now better protected against relay attacks, which leaves about 30% with room for improvement (ADAC keyless security test, in German). A relay attack boosts the signal your smart fob gives off, so a thief can unlock and start the car without touching the key.

ADAC's tests cover European-market models, so check your own owner's manual for what your car does.

A few habits make a thief's job harder:

  • Keep your fob away from exterior doors, outside walls, and windows. ADAC recommends this for keys stored inside buildings.
  • Try a metal tin or a signal-blocking pouch. ADAC says this can work if it truly blocks the signal, and suggests testing it to be sure.
  • Consider a hidden GPS tracker. The National Insurance Crime Bureau lists aftermarket GPS tracking, such as AirTags, among extra layers of security.

Nationally, vehicle thefts fell 23% in 2025 to 659,880 reported thefts, the lowest level in several decades, according to the National Insurance Crime Bureau. Even so, NICB notes that a vehicle is still stolen every 48 seconds.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to reprogram my key fob after replacing the battery?
Often you don't. Many fobs keep their pairing through a battery change, though some vehicles need a re-sync. Check your owner's manual, or call with the make, model, and year.
Can a locksmith program a key fob without going to the dealer?
For many vehicles, yes. A mobile locksmith uses manufacturer procedures where possible and aftermarket programming devices for makes that allow it. A few models need dealer-only PIN codes or a manufacturer's computer, and a good locksmith will say so up front.
What do I do if I lose my only key fob?
Gather your vehicle details and proof of ownership, then call a mobile locksmith. A key can be cut and a new fob programmed at your car. Ask how the old fob is handled, and have a spare made during the same visit, because duplicating a working fob is much simpler than starting from nothing.
Why is my key fob not working after a new battery?
Check the battery type and direction first, then the contacts. Still dead? The fob may need re-pairing, or the chip may be damaged. The 3-Check Fob Test above covers the order.
How do I start my car if the key fob battery is dead?
Many push-button cars have a backup: hold the fob against the start button or a marked spot on the dash or console. The location varies by vehicle, so check your owner's manual first. If the car still won't start, call us.
How long does key fob programming take?
It depends on three things: whether a key blade has to be cut, whether the car accepts programming at the curb or needs dealer-only steps, and whether you still have a working key. Adding a spare when you already have a working fob is the simplest case. If every key is gone, there are more steps. Call with your make, model, and year for a straight answer.
