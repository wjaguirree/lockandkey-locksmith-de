If you own a rental, rekey the locks between tenants. That way the only keys that work are the ones you hand over. You can't know how many copies the last renter made, or who ended up with them. Key Takeaways

  • A rekey between tenants makes past tenants' keys useless.
  • Count keys before and after the job, and write the numbers down.
  • Sound hardware can be rekeyed. Worn or loose hardware should be replaced.
  • Delaware lets a tenant add a lock, with written notice and a key for you.

Why rekey between tenants?

Keys get copied without anyone thinking much about it. One goes to a cousin, a contractor cuts a spare, a ring disappears at the beach. Once the lease ends, all of those keys still open your door. A rekey changes the pins inside the lock, and that's what turns them into scrap.

There's a paperwork benefit too. If something goes wrong later, you can show the old keys stopped working before the new tenant arrived.

What is the Turnover Key Count?

The Turnover Key Count is a habit we suggest to every landlord. For each door, write down how many keys exist before the rekey and how many exist after.

That's two lines per door. On the first line, note what the tenant handed back and what's missing. On the second, note how many keys were cut to the new pattern and who holds each one.

Take a house with a front door, a back door and a garage side door, all on one new key pattern. You'd log three locks and four keys cut, with two staying with you and two going to the new tenant. Keep the sheet in the property file. At the next turnover you begin with a real number, not a guess.

Rekey or replace?

A rekey changes the inside of the lock so a new key works and the old ones don't. A replacement puts a different lock on the door. Our lock rekey page has the full comparison, but in a rental the call mostly depends on how the hardware looks and feels.

What Delaware says about locks and entry

Three parts of Delaware's landlord-tenant code touch a turnover.

Tenants can add a lock. Under 25 Del. C. § 5509, a tenant may install a new lock at their own cost if they tell the landlord in writing, give the landlord a key, the lock fits the system already in place, and the installation doesn't damage the door. So when a tenant leaves, look at every door for a lock you didn't install. Found one? Get its key before you rekey around it.

You need notice to enter. The same section says a landlord gives the tenant at least 48 hours' notice before entering, between 8:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m., except for repairs the tenant asked for and emergencies. That's why we'd book the rekey after the tenant has moved out and handed back the keys. Doing it mid-tenancy means the tenant has to agree or you have to meet the notice rule.

Deposits have a clock. Section 5514 says a landlord who isn't entitled to all of a security deposit has to return it within 20 days of the lease ending. A rekey is an ordinary turnover job, so slot it in with your walkthrough and don't let it drift.

The Delaware Attorney General's office also puts out a landlord-tenant code summary that new tenants receive, and it's worth a read. None of this is legal advice. Your lease and your attorney have the final say.

A turnover checklist

Work through it in this order and the turnover stays quick.

  1. Collect every key from the outgoing tenant and count them.
  2. Walk each door and note any lock that isn't yours.
  3. Try each lock. Does the key turn without a fight, and does the bolt go all the way out?
  4. Choose per door whether to rekey, repair or replace.
  5. Book the locksmith between move-out and the new tenant's first day.
  6. Fill in the Turnover Key Count for every door.
  7. Give the new tenant the new keys only, and write down how many you handed over.
  8. Test every key in its own door before you leave.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to rekey between tenants in Delaware?
The Delaware code sections we read don't say a landlord must rekey at an ordinary turnover. Most landlords do it anyway, because it's the one sure way to kill the old keys. Your lease and your attorney can speak to your own case.
How long does a rekey take?
One standard lock is quick. A house with several doors usually gets done in a single trip. What stretches it is a big door count or hardware in poor shape.
Is rekeying better than changing the locks?
When the hardware is sound, yes. You keep the lock and the old keys stop working. If the lock is worn, loose or damaged, swap it out instead.
Can a tenant change the locks in Delaware?
Yes, within limits. They must tell you in writing and hand you a key, the lock has to fit the system already in place, and it can't damage the door.
Can I put every door on one key?
Usually. If the locks are the same make and in decent shape, we can rekey them to one pattern and a single key opens the lot.
What if a tenant lost a key during the lease?
Rekey once they've left, or earlier if you and the tenant agree. Until the lock is changed, a lost key still opens the door.
