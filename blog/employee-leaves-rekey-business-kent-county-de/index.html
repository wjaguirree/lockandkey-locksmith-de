Do you need to rekey when an employee leaves?

Not always. It turns on what that person could open and whether you know where every copy went. This is how we sort it when you call.

What is the Key Trail Check?

Here's what we run through at the door. We call it the Key Trail Check. Four questions, a pen and about ten minutes.

  1. Who had a key? List every door and every person, including the cleaner and the part-timer.
  2. Where could copies have gone? A key that can be copied at any hardware counter has an unknown number of siblings.
  3. What else opens with a code? Keypads, alarm panels, safes, gate codes and the back-office Wi-Fi closet.
  4. What did we change, and when? Write the date next to each door. That log is your answer if anyone asks later.

Any door where you shrug at question one or two goes on the rekey list. No debate.

Which locks should you rekey first?

Begin with whatever guards the most and would hurt most to lose. For a small shop or office in Kent County the order usually goes like this.

  1. The front and back entry doors. These are the ones everyone has a key to.
  2. The cash, records and stock rooms. Anything with money, personal files or inventory behind it.
  3. Storage units, sheds and yard gates. Easy to forget, and often keyed to a single old padlock.
  4. Company vehicles. If the person drove one, ask about the keys and any spare fobs.
  5. Interior office doors that opened with a master key.

Our lock rekey service swaps the pins inside the cylinder, so a new key works and the old ones don't. The deadbolts and handles stay on the door. Locked out of your own building instead? That's a business lockout call, and it's a different visit.

What about codes, safes and keypads?

This is where owners get burned. The door key comes back on the desk. The code walks out in somebody's head. Two years behind the counter and a person knows the safe combination, the alarm code and the back-door keypad, even if they'd never dream of using them.

Change these the day the person leaves:

  • Keypad door codes. Our keypad entry service covers code changes and checks the power and strike alignment while we are there.
  • Alarm panel codes. Ring your alarm company, since alarm accounts aren't our department.
  • Safe combinations. Our safe lock service includes code changes after staff turnover, once we confirm you own or are authorized to open it.
  • Codes handed to delivery drivers or vendors. Count those as keys too.

Same trap outside the lock world. A 2026 Sagiss offboarding survey, covered by HCA Magazine, found that 82 percent of former employees who had handed back a laptop or other gear still had at least one working account. Handing things back didn't end their access. A returned key ring works the same way if a copy or a code is still floating around. It's a vendor survey about digital accounts, so take it as a nudge, not a measurement of locks. The write-up is on HCA Magazine.

Rekey or replace the hardware?

Mostly, rekey. It's the lighter job and the hardware is usually fine. Replace a lock that's worn, loose, damaged or too old to find parts for. Tired of chasing keys altogether? Access control with keypads or cards is the longer fix.

How do you stop this from happening again?

Skip the binder. Three habits cover most small businesses.

  • Keep a key log. Who, which door, the day it went out, the day it came back. One sheet of paper.
  • Stamp keys "Do Not Duplicate" and use restricted keys for sensitive doors, where the lock supports it. It helps, but it is not a guarantee, so keep the log anyway.
  • Run the Key Trail Check every time someone leaves, not only after a messy exit. Ten minutes. After the second time it's habit.

Frequently asked questions

Should I rekey my business after an employee quits?
If they held keys you can't account for, yes. If every key came back and you've got it written down, the log may be enough. Unsure about an entry door? Rekey it.
Is it better to rekey or replace business locks?
Rekey in most cases. It keeps the hardware you have and makes old keys useless. Replace when the lock is worn or damaged.
How long does rekeying a business take?
One visit for most small businesses. It depends how many doors you have and what cylinders are in them. Describe the building on the phone and we'll tell you what to expect.
Can you rekey a lock without the old key?
Often, yes. We can work from the cylinder itself. We still confirm you own or manage the property first.
What should I do if a fired employee still has a key?
Rekey whatever that key opened, change every code they knew, and jot the date down. Think they might turn up? Call us the same day.
Can you change the combination on a safe?
Yes, once we confirm ownership or authorization. Our safe lock service includes code changes after staff turnover.
