What happens when you lose every key to your car?

More than 95 percent of new North American passenger vehicles had an engine immobilizer as of 2014, according to Locksmith Ledger, and the average U.S. passenger car was 14.5 years old in 2025, per S&P Global Mobility data reported by Repairer Driven News. So most cars on the road need a key matched to the car's computer, not just cut.

All keys lost (AKL) means no working key is left to copy or reprogram from. An immobilizer is a security check between the key and the engine. The key carries a small chip, and the car won't start until it recognizes that chip.

So why does that matter to you? Because a hardware store can't fix this. A new key has to be matched to your car's computer. The good news is that matching a key without an original is routine work for an automotive locksmith, and it doesn't depend on finding the old key first.

What do you need to bring to get a replacement key?

Ford asks for a valid driver's license, state photo ID or passport, plus proof of ownership such as a registration, insurance card, title or bill of sale. Our 4-Document Check applies the same idea to any make: photo ID, proof the car is yours, the VIN, and your location.

Anyone who makes you a key should confirm the car is yours first, and that includes our lost key replacement visits. Other makes vary, so confirm before you go. Here is the 4-Document Check in full:

What if your registration or title was on the key ring that's now gone? Delaware has a path for that. The Delaware DMV handles a duplicate title on form MV213, and every owner on the title must sign. If there's a lien, the lienholder completes a section of the form too. A duplicate registration card requires proof of insurance and your plate number.

A duplicate title takes mailing time, so don't wait on it. Proof of insurance can sometimes stand in for a missing registration card, but ask the locksmith what they accept before you order anything.

If your paperwork was on the lost key ring, we match your photo ID to the VIN on the car and to a record you can pull up that shows your name with that VIN, and we do that before we make a key.

How is a new car key made when you have no original?

A replacement key starts with the VIN, not an old key, as our car key replacement jobs begin. The locksmith verifies ownership, identifies the key, cuts or orders it, programs it to the immobilizer, and tests it. Some makes need more: Volkswagen's tech tip calls for a new control module on 2024 to 2026 Atlas, GTI and Golf R models.

The vehicle identification number tells the locksmith the exact key type, the chip, and the programming method your car uses. From there the work follows a set order.

  1. Verify ownership. Photo ID plus proof the car is yours.
  2. Identify the key. The VIN, make, model, and year narrow down the blank and the chip.
  3. Cut or order the key. A blade is cut to the lock, or a smart key is prepared.
  4. Program it to the car. A programming tool connects to the diagnostic port and adds the new key to the immobilizer.
  5. Remove the lost keys. On most vehicles, the old keys are removed from the car's memory.
  6. Test everything. Start the engine several times, then check the doors, trunk, and remote buttons.

Manufacturers set the rules for this step, and they aren't all the same. Mazda's 2026 CX-70 manual says a replacement key requires security validation, which can add delay, and that you bring all remaining keys to an authorized dealer for the reset (Mazda USA).

That doesn't mean every car is a dealer job. We handle most makes at your car. A few take longer or need extra steps, and we'll tell you which kind yours is on the first call.

Locksmith or dealer: which one fits your situation?

With no spare key, Ford says you must visit a dealer in person with ID and proof of ownership, and the car may need to be towed. A mobile locksmith does the work where the car is parked. Ford adds that a dealer may recommend a locksmith if it can't duplicate the key.

Both can solve an all-keys-lost problem. The difference is where the work happens and how much hauling is involved.

Will your old lost keys still work?

No, not for starting the car. On most vehicles, programming a new key removes the lost keys from the car's memory, so a found key can't start the engine. Mazda's 2026 CX-70 manual describes a dealer reset of the remaining keys and the immobilizer, and says to bring every remaining key. Ask your locksmith to confirm this for your make.

Some makes reach the same result by a different route. Volkswagen's tech tip, covered above, has the control module replaced so the car gets a new immobilizer identity, which VW says eliminates the security vulnerabilities created by the missing keys. Either way, the goal is the same: a key you lost shouldn't be able to start your car again.

That's the part most people worry about, and it's one reason all-keys-lost jobs include a removal step. Before the job ends, ask your locksmith how the lost keys are removed on your car and how you can tell it worked.

If your question is about a dead battery or a fob that needs re-pairing, that's a different problem. Our key fob replacement guide covers it.

Does car insurance cover lost car keys?

Usually not. According to Insurance.com, updated in July 2026, comprehensive coverage applies when a key is damaged in a covered loss such as a fire or vandalism, but it does not pay simply because the key disappeared, and a deductible applies. Check your own policy before you pay anyone.

Plymouth Rock makes a similar point: most policies don't cover lost keys, though some insurers sell key coverage as an add-on, and roadside help may cover a lockout or tow but not the key itself.

So what should you do? Call your agent before you pay anyone. If your keys were stolen along with a bag or a vehicle, the theft claim is a different story, so say so when you call.

Frequently asked questions

What do I do if I lose all my car keys in Delaware?
Gather photo ID, proof the car is yours, and the VIN, then call a mobile locksmith. They'll verify ownership, make a new key, program it to your car, and remove the lost keys. You don't need to find the old key first.
Can a locksmith make a new car key without the original key?
Yes, for most vehicles. The VIN, make, model, and year tell the locksmith which key and chip your car uses. Some makes need a dealer-only procedure, and a good locksmith will say so on the first call.
What proof of ownership do I need for a replacement key in Delaware?
Photo ID plus proof the car is yours, such as a registration, title, or insurance card. Ford's published list also accepts a bill of sale. Requirements vary by company, so confirm what your locksmith or dealer accepts before you go.
Can I get a car key made from the VIN?
The VIN is how the correct key and chip are identified, but a key made from a VIN still has to be programmed to your car. Ownership is checked first.
Will my old lost keys still work after a new one is programmed?
For starting the car, usually not. On most vehicles, programming a new key removes the lost keys from the car's immobilizer memory, so a found key can't start the engine. Ask your locksmith to confirm this for your make, model and year before the job starts.
Does car insurance cover lost car keys?
Usually not. Comprehensive coverage generally applies only when a key is damaged in a covered loss, such as a fire or vandalism, and not when it simply goes missing. Some insurers sell a key add-on, so check your policy.
How do I replace a lost Delaware title or registration?
For a title, the Delaware DMV uses form MV213, signed by all owners, with a lienholder section if there's a lien. For a registration card, the DMV asks for proof of insurance and your plate number. Check the DMV pages for current steps.
Do I need to tow my car to get a key made?
Not usually. A mobile locksmith can make and program a key where the car is parked. Some makes need a dealer-only procedure, and the dealer may ask you to tow the car in. Ask on the first call.
