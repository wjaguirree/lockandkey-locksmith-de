Why get a spare car key now?

A spare is a plan for a bad day. Keys fall out of pockets at the beach, get left on a gas pump counter or go through the wash in a jacket. Most of us only notice we're down to one when it's missing.

Here's the difference between the two situations.

Consumer Reports gives the same advice in its key fob replacement guide: "If you ever find yourself down to one key, order a second one as soon as possible."

What is the Two-Key Rule?

The Two-Key Rule is simple. Always own two working keys or fobs for every car you drive, and know where both of them are.

Why two? Consumer Reports notes that most customer-programmable key fobs need two current, working keys before they'll accept a third. Drop to one and you've lost that shortcut. Drop to zero and you're calling a professional.

Run a quick check on your own household:

  1. Count the working keys for each vehicle.
  2. Write down where each one is kept.
  3. If any car shows a count of one, book a spare.
  4. Re-check after any lost key, new driver or sold car.

A house with three cars should have six keys, and every one of them should be accounted for.

Why does winter make a spare worth having?

Winter doesn't break a key. It does make a missing one a bigger headache.

Short days. It gets dark early. Standing in a dark parking lot with no key is a poor way to spend an evening.

Idling cars. The Delaware State Police have warned drivers that a large majority of vehicle thefts they looked at happened at gas station and convenience store lots, with the car left idling and unlocked. Title 21, section 4182 of the Delaware Code says a driver leaving a car unattended must stop the engine, lock the ignition, remove the key and set the brake. Warm-up habits and a spare key that is safely stored go together, because a second key you can use beats leaving the first one in the car.

Fob batteries. Small coin batteries do weaker work in the cold. If your fob has started needing two presses, a fresh battery before the first freeze is worth doing, and it's a good moment to ask about a spare.

Holiday travel. Out-of-town guests, borrowed cars and busy weekends are exactly when a key goes missing.

Where should a spare live?

AAA suggests keeping a spare somewhere separate from your key ring, such as a kitchen drawer, or handing one to someone you trust. We agree with the first part. One spot to skip is the inside of the car or a magnetic box under the bumper, since anyone who finds it has your vehicle.
